Research

Our research seeks to understand and predict interactions among land, water, atmosphere, and human systems across timescales ranging from weather to climate. We develop and apply observations, land-surface models, Earth-system models, and artificial intelligence to improve prediction of hydrologic extremes, water resources, and environmental change.

Research Themes

  • Land-atmosphere interactions: Understanding how soil moisture, vegetation, snow, groundwater, and surface fluxes influence weather, climate, and hydroclimate extremes.
  • Hydrology and water resources: Improving prediction of droughts, floods, streamflow, reservoir evaporation, and water availability.
  • Land surface modeling: Developing and applying models such as Noah-MP and CLM to improve representation of water, energy, carbon, vegetation, snow, and groundwater processes.
  • Earth system predictability: Investigating sources of predictability from subseasonal to annual timescales, with emphasis on land memory and coupled land-atmosphere processes.
  • Extreme events: Studying droughts, floods, heat waves, freezing rain, and compound hazards affecting communities, infrastructure, and water systems.
  • AI for Earth system science: Using machine learning and physics-guided AI to diagnose model errors, improve prediction, and integrate observations with process-based models.
  • Infrastructure resilience and climate risk: Connecting environmental prediction with energy, water, and infrastructure resilience planning.

Selected Current Directions

  • NASA-supported research on water resources, land modeling, and prediction.
  • DOE GRID-AWARE research on compound hazards, Earth-system predictability, and infrastructure resilience.
  • Texas water-resources studies involving streamflow, reservoir evaporation, drought, and hydrologic extremes.
  • Freezing-rain and winter-weather hazards affecting infrastructure systems.
